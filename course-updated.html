How Credit Scores Work

Understand the formula behind your score so you can play the game strategically.

What Is a Credit Score?

A credit score is a number between 300 and 850 that tells lenders how likely you are to repay money you borrow. It's calculated by credit bureaus — Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion — using your credit report data.

The most widely used model is the FICO Score. Think of it like a grade — except it follows you into apartments, car loans, and eventually mortgages.

Score Ranges

RangeRatingWhat It Means
300–579PoorHard to get approved for anything
580–669FairSome approval, high interest rates
670–739GoodMost lenders will approve you
740–799Very GoodBetter rates and terms
800–850ExceptionalBest rates available anywhere
